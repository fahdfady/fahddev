So starting right into the point, That's the pattern:

- **I get so excited to read a book**, genuinely believing this book will have good effect on me
- **I read the first chapter**, I get even more excited
- **Second chapter is a bit boring**, I skim through it and don't dive into it that much
- **Third chapter *is* the most exciting chapter**, and mostly the one i'm always anticipating. It's always the one with the first hands-on work I can apply myself alongside the book. I get through it, get my hand dirty a bit, and then get overwhelmed and lose track of what I am doing.

You can apply this to this set of books I was reading recently:

- Linkers and Loaders
- Designing Data Intensive Applications
- Calculus (Michael Spivak)

All of these books have the exact same pattern. For Linkers and loaders ch3 was the real discussion about linkers and different executable formats. For DDIA it was implementing a database (reaching B-trees and stuff). For Calculus it was the functions (I'm interested in Functional Programming and I want to understand the Maths behind it, so of course I was curios on Spivak's approach explaining it)

Of course being Gen Z and brainrotten and all that bla bla bla helps in painting that picture. I actually have trouble reading. Like.. reading anything at this point.

But, I believe in reading and it's effect on the mind and the experiences it encapsulates on the desired level of deepness

I don't have time to commit now. I have a lot of research and work regarding MetaSSR, plus that I am working a fulltime SWE job. And fortunately I'm a human and have a life to live and responsibilities to meet.

I see myself after GSoC taking a break and rearranging everything to commit to *only one*  book. Maybe I can do that now and I'm just too lazy?
